Hardness: what Denver Water publishes

The Denver Water 2026 Water Quality Report (calendar-year 2025 sampling) lists Total Hardness in the distribution-system secondary / informational parameters table:

  • Average: 105,009 ppb (that is 105.009 mg/L as CaCO₃)
  • Range: 55,000–124,000 ppb
  • Sampled quarterly

Those published numbers are why hardnessMgL is set in frontmatter. SoftenerIndex does not invent a grains-per-gallon figure from that average — the PDF does not print gpg — and does not collapse the quarterly range into a fake single “set-point” SoftenerIndex made up.

Denver Water’s hardness page adds qualitative context SoftenerIndex will not turn into grains:

  • System water is described as soft to moderately hard.
  • The southern collection system is called moderately hard; the northern collection system is called somewhat soft.
  • Winter tends to run slightly harder; late-spring through fall runoff tends to dilute minerals.

USGS classification language on that page (soft under 60 mg/L; hard 121–180 mg/L) is context for reading the report’s average and range — not a SoftenerIndex hardness invention.

If you are on a suburb utility, a special district, or a private well, pull that system’s current CCR or test the tap before you size resin. Denver Water’s average is not your neighbor’s set-point.

Finding an installer without a fake roster

Use the Colorado DORA / DPO license lookup (linked from the State Plumbing Board licensing services page). Confirm:

  • The legal name matches the quote.
  • The license type covers the work (plumbers / applicable contractor credentials).
  • The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through How to read a dealer quote. Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input (the Denver Water average / range as context, or your own test). Compare more than one written bid using the 2026 fair-quote guide.

If the pitch is a “salt-free softener,” read salt-based vs salt-free before you sign. Door-to-door is not a credential.

Local questions that are not invented numbers

Ask Denver-area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

  • Where will the tank sit (garage slab, basement, exterior), and what freeze exposure does that imply at elevation?
  • How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed local rules for brine discharge?
  • Are you on Denver Water, another Front Range utility, or a well — and which hardness input did they use?
  • Who pulls any required permit?
