Quote literacy

How to read a water-softener dealer quote

A line-by-line checklist for dealer quotes: equipment identity, capacity, labor, exclusions, and pressure tactics — without invented price benchmarks.

Updated September 21, 2026By Bob Molina

A dealer quote is a contract preview. If you cannot tell what is being sold, you cannot tell whether the price is fair — and SoftenerIndex will not invent a benchmark price for you to lean on.

Print the quote, or open the PDF, and walk these sections in order. Anything you cannot find is an open question, not a yes.

1. Who is contracting

  • Legal business name, not only a DBA on a magnet.
  • License number for the trade that will touch your plumbing (in Arizona, start with the Registrar of Contractors public lookup — we do not fill city pages with unverified rosters).
  • Address and a way to reach someone who is not only a mobile closer.
  • Whether the installer is the dealer or a subcontractor.

If the quote is a first name and a cell number, you do not have a dealer quote yet.

2. What machine is on the page

Demand the identity of the product:

  • Brand and model, not “the Platinum 9000” unless that is the model.
  • Certification marks the dealer claims (for example NSF/ANSI 44 for cation exchange softeners) and whether this model is on the listing, not a cousin.
  • Resin volume (cubic feet) and rated grain capacity at a stated efficiency, if they cite one.
  • Metered / demand-initiated regeneration vs. timed regeneration.
  • Bypass valve included or not.
  • Brine tank size and whether grid / safety float is included.

If capacity is missing, the quote is not sized. If the brand is missing, you are buying a story.

Culligan and Kinetico usually name dealer packages; Fleck and Clack usually name control valves that assemblers put on tanks. Decode those four names in Culligan / Kinetico / Fleck / Clack brand decode before you treat a valve nickname as a finished softener.

3. What water it was sized for

Look for:

  • Hardness as grains per gallon or mg/L as calcium carbonate, from a test or a named utility report.
  • Iron, manganese, pH, or TDS if they claim those affect the bid.
  • Number of people or a peak-flow assumption.
  • Compensation for high hardness (reserve capacity) explained in words, not a wink.

If hardness is blank, the dealer guessed or plans to upsell after a “free test” that you never see in writing. SoftenerIndex city pages follow the same rule: no hardness figure without a source. See methodology.

4. The money lines, still without our own numbers

You do not need our invented averages to read their arithmetic. You need their arithmetic to be labeled:

Line What you should see
Equipment Model and quantity
Installation labor Hours or a lump that is named as labor
Additional plumbing Pipe, fittings, reroutes, haul-away of an old unit
Drain / pump / electrical Called out if needed
Permit / inspection Included, excluded, or “owner’s responsibility”
Tax Stated
Service / salt / membership Optional and separable
Finance APR, term, and the cash price beside it

A single lump “installed” figure is legal in many places. It is also how extras hide. Ask for a breakdown even if they will not change the total.

Watch for exclusions in the footnotes: “unforeseen copper replacement,” “electrical by others,” “permit extra,” “software subscription for the app valve.” Those are still costs.

5. Warranty vs. who shows up

  • Valve warranty years, resin years, brine tank years — separately.
  • Whether warranty is manufacturer, dealer, or both, and whether it requires their salt or their annual visit.
  • Response: who comes if it fails on a holiday, and whether that is a billable trip.

A long warranty on a house-brand valve with no spare-parts story is a paragraph, not protection.

6. Pressure tells (not prices)

None of these prove the quote is high or low. They prove the process is designed to stop comparison:

  • The discount expires when the closer leaves.
  • Financing is presented before the cash price.
  • You are asked to sign a work order that is not the same document as the quote.
  • “This resin is medical grade” or similar language with no standard attached.
  • Salt-free is sold as a softener. It usually is not. Read salt-based vs salt-free.

7. Compare two quotes only on matching scope

You cannot compare last lines until:

  • Same (or explicitly different) capacity and resin volume.
  • Same drain and electrical assumptions.
  • Same permit handling.
  • Same whether an existing softener is removed.

Then pick on equipment identity, contract terms, and a dealer you can find after Saturday. Use the 2026 fair-quote guide for the cost stack; use this page as the highlighter.
