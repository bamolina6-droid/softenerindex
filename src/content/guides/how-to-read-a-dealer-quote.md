A dealer quote is a contract draft. Read it like one. The goal is not to memorize industry slang; it is to see which obligations are written down and which are still in the air.

This page is a reading order. It does not tell you what the job “should” cost.

## 1. Who is on the letterhead

Find the legal business name, address, and the license number you can look up. A truck wrap and a first name are not the contracting party. If the quote is from a national call center, ask who actually pulls the permit and whose insurance covers the crawlspace.

For Arizona work, use the license lookup linked from the [Phoenix city file](/cities/phoenix-az). Other cities should get the same treatment when those pages exist.

## 2. What equipment is actually specified

Highlight every product line and ask whether it is a **model** or a **category**. You want:

- Control valve make and model
- Mineral tank diameter and height, or an unambiguous SKU
- Media (resin or otherwise) type and cubic feet if stated
- Brine tank or other consumable vessel
- Bypass valve
- Any pre-filter, UV, carbon, or RO add-on, each on its own line

If two quotes cannot be lined up model-to-model, you are not comparing prices. You are comparing stories.

## 3. What the installer will physically do

The labor paragraph should survive a change of salesperson. Look for:

- Point of connection (main inlet, garage, hall closet)
- Drain or air-gap details
- Electrical (existing outlet vs new circuit)
- Floor protection, haul-away, and whether the old unit is removed
- Start-up, programming, and a walkthrough

“Turnkey” with no list is how extra drain runs become invoices. Extra work can be legitimate; it should be priced as a line, not discovered after the deposit.

## 4. Water data the quote depends on

A sizing paragraph that does not mention **your** hardness measurement is incomplete. The quote should say where the number came from (lab, test strip, utility report) and the date. SoftenerIndex will not fill in a hardness value for you. If the dealer filled one in without a test, treat it as unverified.

Iron, manganese, pH, and TDS are not vanity extras on well water. If you are on a well, the quote should say so, and the equipment list should match that story.

## 5. Warranty, service, and what voids them

Circle the words **parts**, **labor**, **resin**, **workmanship**, and **lifetime**. Ask what event ends each one (sale of the house, missed salt, missed annual service, a DIY bypass). Get the warranty issuer (factory vs dealer) in writing.

A service plan is optional. A service plan that is the only way the warranty pays is not optional — it is part of the price, even if we do not print a dollar amount here.

## 6. Money: cash price vs financed price

Before you look at a monthly figure, find:

- Cash price of equipment and of installation, separately
- Sales tax handling
- Deposit, progress payments, and final payment trigger
- Whether financing is dealer-arranged
- APR, term, origination fees, and prepayment rules if financed

If the packet has a payment and no APR, you do not yet have the cost of money. Stop there.
