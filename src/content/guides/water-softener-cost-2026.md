A fair quote is a document you can compare. It is not a number spoken at the kitchen table, and it is not a “today only” total that hides equipment, labor, drain work, and financing in one line.

SoftenerIndex does not publish a 2026 “average installed cost.” Those figures are easy to invent and hard to defend. If a sourced, dated range is ever added, it will appear in this page’s frontmatter (`published_price_low`, `published_price_high`, `price_source_url`, `price_verified_on`) and in the sourced-figure box above. Until then, use the structure below.

## What a fair quote has to separate

Ask the dealer to split at least these rows. If they will not, you do not have a quote you can check.

| Line | What it should name | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment | Valve brand and model, tank size, resin type, brine tank | “System” is not a model number. |
| Capacity basis | Grain rating and how it was sized | A rating without your hardness test is a guess. |
| Installation labor | Hours or a fixed install package, including start-up | Labor buried in equipment is how totals become incomparable. |
| Plumbing changes | Drain, saddle, electrical, bypass, relocation | Hard-water homes differ; the quote should say what is in and out. |
| Removal | Old softener or neglected bypass | Silence here becomes a change order. |
| Permits / inspection | Who pulls them, who pays | Do not assume the city is free or included. |
| Warranty | Parts vs labor vs resin vs workmanship, in writing | Verbal “lifetime” is not a term. |
| Consumables | Salt, media, pre-filters — who supplies the first fill | Recurring cost is part of the purchase even if it is not in the financed total. |
| Financing | APR, term, amount financed, fees — or “cash only” | A low monthly number is not a price. |

Take that table to the appointment. If the packet you leave with cannot fill the left column, it is a pitch, not a quote.

## Size the job from water, not from the brochure

Capacity talk is where door-to-door scripts run ahead of data. A grain rating is a claim about how much hardness the unit can exchange between regenerations. It is only as honest as:

1. A hardness measurement from **your** tap or a lab, not a regional anecdote.
2. An honest count of people and fixtures the dealer used in the sizing story.
3. Whether the dealer sized for compensated hardness (iron and manganese change the picture) or ignored it.

SoftenerIndex will not tell you a household default in gallons or a city-wide grains-per-gallon number. Read your utility’s Consumer Confidence Report, or test the tap, then make the dealer show the arithmetic on the quote. For Phoenix municipal customers, start with the [Phoenix city file](/cities/phoenix-az).

## Salt-based, salt-free, and “conditioners”

Cost conversations collapse when the product category is fuzzy. Ion-exchange softeners, template-assisted crystallization devices, carbon filters, and reverse osmosis are not interchangeable, and they should not share a single price story. Read [salt-based vs salt-free](/guides/salt-based-vs-salt-free) before you compare two totals that are not the same machine.

If a quote says “softener” and the spec sheet does not exchange hardness ions, ask them to retitle the line. Mislabeling is a cost problem because you cannot compare warranties or salt budgets across categories.

## Pressure, timing, and financing

Fair pricing is slow. Unfair pricing is urgent.

Treat these as process failures, not as discounts you missed:

- The total is only available if you sign before the representative leaves.
- The company will not leave a written packet or model numbers.
- The monthly payment is presented before the cash price and the APR.
- The “free” equipment is bundled with a service contract you have not read.
- The installer is “a national brand” with no local license number on the paperwork.

None of those require a dollar figure to evaluate. They are about whether you can still compare the job tomorrow.

## How to use this guide in 2026

1. Read [how to read a dealer quote](/guides/how-to-read-a-dealer-quote) and print the checklist.
2. Get water data from a public report or a test — not from the salesperson’s memory.
3. Collect two written quotes that use the same line-item map.
4. Confirm license status in your state before you pay a deposit.
5. Ignore any “market average” that does not cite a date and a document. We ignore those too.
