Denver is SoftenerIndex’s Front Range city page: a housing market where scale talk, soap performance, and softener pitches show up — but where **which Denver Water collection system and season** matter more than a blog’s “Denver is X grains” claim. That is not a SoftenerIndex installer count or market price. Hardness appears in the data box only because Denver Water’s current Water Quality Report prints an average Total Hardness figure SoftenerIndex can cite. Installer rows stay empty until a Colorado license lookup backs each name.

## Listing status: research

This page is in `research` status. The `installers` array in frontmatter is empty on purpose. We will not invent a “top Denver water softener companies” list, scrape map-pack directories, or guess how many dealers serve the metro.

When a licensed plumber or plumbing company is added, each row must include a name and the Colorado Division of Professions and Occupations (DORA / DPO) public lookup it was verified against. Until then, treat every door-to-door pitch as unverified.

## Hardness: what Denver Water publishes

The [Denver Water 2026 Water Quality Report](https://www.denverwater.org/sites/default/files/water-quality-report-2026.pdf) (calendar-year **2025** sampling) lists **Total Hardness** in the distribution-system secondary / informational parameters table:

- **Average: 105,009 ppb** (that is **105.009 mg/L** as CaCO₃)
- **Range: 55,000–124,000 ppb**
- Sampled **quarterly**

Those published numbers are why `hardnessMgL` is set in frontmatter. SoftenerIndex does **not** invent a grains-per-gallon figure from that average — the PDF does not print gpg — and does not collapse the quarterly range into a fake single “set-point” SoftenerIndex made up.

[Denver Water’s hardness page](https://www.denverwater.org/your-water/water-quality/water-hardness) adds qualitative context SoftenerIndex will not turn into grains:

- System water is described as **soft to moderately hard**.
- The **southern** collection system is called **moderately hard**; the **northern** collection system is called **somewhat soft**.
- Winter tends to run slightly harder; late-spring through fall runoff tends to dilute minerals.

USGS classification language on that page (soft under 60 mg/L; hard 121–180 mg/L) is context for reading the report’s average and range — not a SoftenerIndex hardness invention.

If you are on a **suburb utility**, a **special district**, or a **private well**, pull that system’s current CCR or test the tap before you size resin. Denver Water’s average is not your neighbor’s set-point.

## Finding an installer without a fake roster

Use the [Colorado DORA / DPO license lookup](https://apps2.colorado.gov/dora/licensing/Lookup/LicenseLookup.aspx) (linked from the [State Plumbing Board licensing services](https://dpo.colorado.gov/Plumbing/LicensingServices) page). Confirm:

- The legal name matches the quote.
- The license type covers the work (plumbers / applicable contractor credentials).
- The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through [How to read a dealer quote](/guides/how-to-read-a-dealer-quote). Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input (the Denver Water average / range as context, or your own test). Compare more than one written bid using the [2026 fair-quote guide](/guides/water-softener-cost-2026).

If the pitch is a “salt-free softener,” read [salt-based vs salt-free](/guides/salt-based-vs-salt-free) before you sign. Door-to-door is not a credential.

## Local questions that are not invented numbers

Ask Denver-area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

- Where will the tank sit (garage slab, basement, exterior), and what freeze exposure does that imply at elevation?
- How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed local rules for brine discharge?
- Are you on Denver Water, another Front Range utility, or a well — and which hardness input did they use?
- Who pulls any required permit?
