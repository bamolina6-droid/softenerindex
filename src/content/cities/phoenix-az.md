## How to read Phoenix water quality yourself

Start with the City of Phoenix Water Services water-quality page linked above. Look for the current Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) or equivalent drinking-water quality report for **your** system. Phoenix is not a single tap. Pressure zones, blended sources, and well vs. surface supply can differ by neighborhood.

When you open the report, hunt for hardness (sometimes listed as calcium carbonate, grains, or ppm). Copy the figure onto your quote worksheet with the report date. Do not let a salesperson replace that document with a regional story. If the report does not list hardness in a form you can use, say so on the worksheet and get a tap test.

SoftenerIndex will not transcribe a number from memory onto this page.

## Municipal vs. well

City water and private wells are different jobs. A Phoenix municipal quote that never mentions drain location, meter, and existing shutoffs is incomplete. A well quote that never mentions iron, bacteria testing, or pressure tanks is incomplete. Ask the dealer to write which supply they sized for.

Desert installs also raise ordinary practical questions — shade, heat on outdoor equipment, and brine discharge — that belong in the labor paragraph, not in a slogan. We will not invent a permit fee or a “typical Phoenix install.” Check the city and the Arizona Registrar of Contractors for who must be licensed for the work described.

## Finding a local pro without the door pitch

1. Read [how to read a dealer quote](/guides/how-to-read-a-dealer-quote) first.
2. Look up the company on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors site linked above **before** a deposit.
3. Collect a written packet you can take home.
4. Compare category and model, not a kitchen-table total. The [2026 cost guide](/guides/water-softener-cost-2026) and [salt-based vs salt-free](/guides/salt-based-vs-salt-free) pages are the comparison frame.

If someone knocks uninvited, you are not required to provide a hardness number, a utility bill, or a same-day decision. SoftenerIndex’s tagline is the rule of thumb: **hard water, fair prices, local pros — not a door-to-door quote.**
