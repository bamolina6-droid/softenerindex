Hardness: how to get a real figure

Do not use a blog’s “Albuquerque is X grains.” ABCWUA says hardness depends on the source blend and publishes zone-level water-quality tables — not a SoftenerIndex citywide grain count on this research page. SoftenerIndex leaves hardnessGpg and hardnessMgL unset.

To get a number you can hand a dealer:

  1. Open ABCWUA’s drinking-water FAQs for the utility’s own hardness language and the official PPM-to-grains conversion (divide PPM by 17.1).
  2. Use the Water Quality by Distribution Zone map: search your address, open your zone’s report, and read hardness only if that table prints it for your zone (or a labeled citywide average the utility itself publishes).
  3. If the published document does not state hardness for your zone in gpg or mg/L (PPM) as CaCO₃, do not convert adjectives (“moderately hard,” “hard”) into grains.
  4. A test at your tap (titration kit or lab) is the figure that actually sizes a resin bed. Zone tables are context, not a substitute for your address.

When we attach a figure, it will appear in the data box on this page with the source name, URL, and retrieved date. If that box still says “not sourced,” there is no SoftenerIndex hardness number.

Finding an installer without a fake roster

Use the New Mexico Regulation & Licensing Department verify-a-license hub and the linked PSI Construction Industries Division search. Confirm:

  • The legal name matches the quote.
  • The license classification covers the work (mechanical/plumbing classifications as applicable under CID rules).
  • The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through How to read a dealer quote. Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input. Compare more than one written bid using the 2026 fair-quote guide.

Door-to-door is common in hard-water markets. It is not a credential.

Local questions that are not numbers

Ask Albuquerque-area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

  • Where will the tank sit (garage slab, closet, exterior), and what freeze / sun / dust exposure does that imply at altitude?
  • How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed the utility / sewer district will accept brine?
  • Is the pitch salt-based ion exchange or a salt-free conditioner? Read salt-based vs salt-free if the language is muddy.
  • Who pulls any required permit?
  • If you run an evaporative cooler, what maintenance does the bidder assume — ABCWUA’s own FAQs treat TDS residue on cooler pads as a separate household issue from “how hard is my water?”
