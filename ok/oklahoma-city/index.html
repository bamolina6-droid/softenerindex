Hardness: how to get a real figure

Do not use a blog’s “Oklahoma City is X grains.” Aggregators disagree wildly, and SoftenerIndex will not average them into a fake citywide number. SoftenerIndex leaves hardnessGpg and hardnessMgL unset because the current OCWUT Drinking Water Quality Report does not publish total hardness for SoftenerIndex to cite.

To get a number you can hand a dealer:

  1. Open the current CCR (and the ODEQ copy for PWS OK1020902) and read only what the utility actually prints. If there is still no hardness table in gpg or mg/L as CaCO₃, do not invent one from adjectives or from turbidity footnotes about plant softening chemistry.
  2. Remember supply can blend across five surface reservoirs and two treatment plants — a citywide blog number is not your pressure zone.
  3. A test at your tap (titration kit or lab) is the figure that actually sizes a resin bed. Utility reports are context, not a substitute for your address.
  4. If OCWUT later publishes an explicit hardness table for a stated plant, zone, or system average, SoftenerIndex can attach it as hardnessSource — not before.

When we attach a figure, it will appear in the data box on this page with the source name, URL, and retrieved date. If that box still says “not sourced,” there is no SoftenerIndex hardness number.

Finding an installer without a fake roster

Use the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board “Are They Licensed?” tools and the plumbing trade pages. Confirm:

  • The legal name matches the quote.
  • The license class covers the plumbing work (softener installs that cut into supply lines).
  • The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through How to read a dealer quote. Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input. Compare more than one written bid using the 2026 fair-quote guide.

Door-to-door is common in hard-water markets. It is not a credential.

Local questions that are not numbers

Ask Oklahoma City–area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

  • Where will the tank sit (garage slab, closet, exterior), and what freeze / storm exposure does that imply?
  • How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed the utility / sewer rules will accept brine?
  • Is the pitch salt-based ion exchange or a salt-free conditioner? Read salt-based vs salt-free if the language is muddy.
  • Who pulls any required local permit?
  • If your neighborhood is on a wholesale or special-service arrangement (the CCR notes Edmond water for The Falls), which utility’s quality report and rules actually apply?
