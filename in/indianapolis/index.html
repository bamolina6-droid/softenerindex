Hardness: how to get a real figure

Do not use a blog’s “Indianapolis is X grains.” SoftenerIndex publishes a figure only when a named utility table is attached as hardnessSource.

For Indianapolis (PWS IN5249004) and Morgan County (IN5255019), Citizens Energy Group’s 2025 Water Quality Data lists Hardness (ppm) under SECONDARY DRINKING WATER STANDARDS & UNREGULATED CONTAMINANTS (Sampled at Treatment Plant):

  • Average of All Samples: 317 ppm / 19 grains/gal
  • Maximum of All Samples: 460 ppm / 27 grains/gal
  • System Wide Range: 148–460 ppm / 8.7–27 grains/gal

Those averages are the SoftenerIndex hardnessMgL (317) and hardnessGpg (19) values. The Drinking Water Report hub FAQ also notes hardness expressed as calcium carbonate averages about 320 mg/L (19 gpg) and can vary by the plant serving your address — call 317-924-3311 for address-specific guidance before programming a softener.

To get a number you can hand a dealer:

  1. Open the Indianapolis & Morgan County 2025 tables and read the Hardness row yourself — confirm units and that your service area matches IN5249004 / IN5255019 (not a different Citizens Westfield system).
  2. Remember supply blends surface water (White River, Fall Creek, Eagle Creek Reservoir) and groundwater plants; a system average is not your pressure zone.
  3. A test at your tap (titration kit or lab) is the figure that actually sizes a resin bed. Utility tables are context, not a substitute for your address.

When the data box on this page shows a sourced hardness figure, it points at the Citizens table above. If that box ever says “not sourced,” there is no SoftenerIndex hardness number.

Finding an installer without a fake roster

Use the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency license verification search. Confirm:

  • The legal name matches the quote.
  • The license type covers the plumbing work (softener installs that cut into supply lines).
  • The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through How to read a dealer quote. Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input. Compare more than one written bid using the 2026 fair-quote guide.

Door-to-door is common in hard-water markets. It is not a credential.

Local questions that are not numbers

Ask Indianapolis-area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

  • Where will the tank sit (basement, garage slab, closet, exterior), and what freeze / humidity exposure does that imply for Central Indiana winters?
  • How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed sewer / utility rules will accept brine?
  • Is the pitch salt-based ion exchange or a salt-free conditioner? Read salt-based vs salt-free if the language is muddy.
  • Who pulls any required local permit?
  • Which Citizens treatment plant / pressure zone serves the address, and did the bidder use your tap hardness (or the utility’s address-specific figure) rather than a blog number?
