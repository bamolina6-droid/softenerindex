Hardness: how to get a real figure

Do not use a blog’s “San Antonio is X grains.” Aggregators disagree, and SoftenerIndex will not average them into a fake citywide number.

What SoftenerIndex does cite from SAWS:

  • The Water Quality FAQs answer to “What is hardness?” states that typical hardness in water provided by SAWS ranges from 15 to 20 gpg, expressed as calcium carbonate from Edwards and Trinity aquifer minerals.
  • SoftenerIndex leaves hardnessGpg and hardnessMgL unset because that FAQ is a range, not a single SoftenerIndex frontmatter figure, and inventing a midpoint would violate the project rules.

The current Main System CCR PDF (2024 data, PWS TX0150018) documents regulated contaminants and source mix (Edwards, Carrizo, Simsboro, Trinity, Wilcox aquifers; some Canyon Lake surface water; purchased-water partners). It states secondary constituents are available on Texas Drinking Water Viewer and does not print a total-hardness table SoftenerIndex can copy into frontmatter. Older archive CCRs that once listed hardness under secondary tables are not reused as “current” SoftenerIndex numbers.

To get a number you can hand a dealer:

  1. Read the SAWS FAQ hardness answer and the current Main System CCR yourself — confirm units and that your service is Main System TX0150018 (not a satellite SAWS system with its own CCR).
  2. Remember supply can blend aquifers and purchased-water partners — a metro blog number is not your pressure zone.
  3. A test at your tap (titration kit or lab) is the figure that actually sizes a resin bed. Utility ranges are context, not a substitute for your address.
  4. If SAWS later publishes an explicit single hardness average (or plant/zone table) SoftenerIndex can attach as hardnessSource, those fields can fill — not before.

When we attach a figure, it will appear in the data box on this page with the source name, URL, and retrieved date. If that box still says “not sourced,” there is no SoftenerIndex single hardness number (the FAQ range still lives in the note above).

Finding an installer without a fake roster

For contracted softener work in Texas, start with:

  1. TCEQ licensing search and the Water Treatment Specialist overview — WTS licensing applies to many contracted potable-treatment installs; licensed plumbers are exempt from the WTS requirement.
  2. TSBPE Public License Search when the bidder claims plumber status.

Confirm:

  • The legal name matches the quote.
  • The license type covers the work (WTS and/or plumbing, as applicable).
  • The license is active on the day you sign.

Then run the quote through How to read a dealer quote. Demand brand, model, grain capacity, and a hardness input. Compare more than one written bid using the 2026 fair-quote guide.

Door-to-door is common in hard-water markets. It is not a credential.

Local questions that are not numbers

Ask San Antonio–area bidders things that do not require SoftenerIndex to invent data:

  • Where will the tank sit (garage slab, closet, exterior), and what heat / freeze exposure does that imply for South Texas summers and occasional cold snaps?
  • How will regeneration reach a drain, and has the bidder confirmed SAWS / sewer rules will accept brine?
  • Is the pitch salt-based ion exchange or a salt-free conditioner? Read salt-based vs salt-free if the language is muddy.
  • Who pulls any required local permit?
  • Did the bidder use your tap hardness (or SAWS’s stated 15–20 gpg typical range as context only) rather than a blog number, and which SAWS system / pressure zone serves the address?
